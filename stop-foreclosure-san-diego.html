The timeline, locally

How foreclosure moves in San Diego County

It starts at the County Recorder

Like the rest of California, San Diego County foreclosures are non-judicial: no courtroom, just recorded notices and deadlines. The Notice of Default recorded with the San Diego County Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk opens a 90-day window in which you have the legal right to reinstate the loan and stop everything.

Then a sale date — with at least 21 days' notice

After the 90 days, a Notice of Trustee's Sale sets an auction at least 21 days out, at the time and place printed on the notice. From first missed payment to auction is typically several months, but the calendar only moves in one direction unless someone acts.

Why equity changes the math here

In a market like San Diego's, the difference between a rushed auction and a properly handled resolution can be a life-changing amount of money. Equity gives you leverage — for a modification, for a postponement, or for a sale on your terms before the sale date. Walk through the full sequence on the California foreclosure timeline.

Resources worth a second opinion

HUD-approved housing counselors — free

(800) 569-4287 connects you with HUD-approved counseling agencies serving San Diego County, at no cost. Use them. Multiple sources of good information is how you spot the bad actors who target homeowners in default.

San Diego Superior Court self-help

The San Diego Superior Court runs free self-help centers that can explain legal procedures, and the Legal Aid Society of San Diego offers free civil legal services to eligible county residents, including housing matters.
