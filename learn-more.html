How it works

First understand the clock. Then beat it.

Foreclosure in California runs on a legal timeline with real minimums built into it. Once you can see where you are on that timeline, the panic gets smaller and the options get clearer.

The California foreclosure timeline

Months 1–3+ · Behind on payments

Nothing is recorded yet

Servicers generally must contact you and wait at least 120 days of delinquency before starting foreclosure. This is the quietest, cheapest moment to act — catch up, restructure, or sell at full market value with your title still clean.

Day 1 · Notice of Default recorded

The formal clock starts

The NOD opens a statutory reinstatement period of at least three months. Pay the arrears and fees during this window and the foreclosure is cancelled outright. It's also prime time to submit a complete loan modification application, which the servicer must review.

After ~90 days · Notice of Trustee's Sale

An auction date exists — and still moves

The sale can happen no sooner than 21 days after the notice. Dates get postponed routinely for active offers, complete modification files, and short-sale packages. A date on the calendar is leverage to work with, not a verdict.

Auction day and after

Even post-sale, there are moves

If the home sold for more than what was owed, the surplus belongs to you and must be claimed. Relocation assistance is negotiable, occupancy timelines have rules, and sales with procedural defects can be challenged. Nobody should walk away from money they're owed.
