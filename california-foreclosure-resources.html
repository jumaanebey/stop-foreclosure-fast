California foreclosure resources

Every legitimate source of free help, in one place

I keep this list because good information from more than one source is how you avoid the scams that circle foreclosure. Everything below is free or government-sponsored — use them, alongside me or instead of me.

The one rule that matters

Legitimate foreclosure help is free. California law prohibits charging homeowners upfront fees for foreclosure assistance. Anyone demanding money before doing the work is breaking the law — walk away and report them.

Government assistance

Federal agency

HUD Housing Counseling

The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development funds free housing counseling agencies throughout California. Counselors help you understand your options, review your finances, communicate with your lender, and submit loan modification applications. This is the first call I recommend to everyone.

Phone: (800) 569-4287 (toll-free)
Website: HUD.gov/findacounselor
Federal program

FHA Loss Mitigation

If you have an FHA loan, you may qualify for FHA-specific loss mitigation options, including modification programs and partial claims. Contact your loan servicer and ask specifically about FHA options.

FHA Resource Center: (800) 225-5342
Website: HUD.gov FHA Resources
Federal program

VA Loan Assistance

Veterans with VA-guaranteed loans have access to VA loan technicians who can intervene with your servicer on your behalf. The VA offers several loss mitigation options of its own.

VA Loan Hotline: (877) 827-3702
Website: VA.gov Home Loans
State program

California Mortgage Relief Program

The state's pandemic-era grant program paid past-due mortgage and property tax balances for eligible homeowners. It is no longer accepting new applications, but the website remains the official source if the state announces new funding — and it's worth checking rather than trusting anyone who "guarantees" you a grant.

Website: CaMortgageRelief.org

Know your rights

California Homeowner Bill of Rights

California law gives homeowners facing foreclosure real, enforceable protections:

  • No dual tracking: your servicer cannot move the foreclosure forward while your complete loan modification application is under review
  • Single point of contact: you're entitled to one person or team handling your case
  • Written decisions: a modification denial must be explained in writing
  • Right to appeal: you can appeal a denied loan modification

Unsure what these mean for your stage? See the California foreclosure timeline or the plain-English glossary.

How to spot a foreclosure scam

Scammers pull foreclosure notices from public records and target the people on them. Red flags:

  • Charging upfront fees before providing any service
  • Guaranteeing to stop your foreclosure
  • Asking you to sign over your deed
  • Telling you to stop communicating with your lender
  • Asking you to make your mortgage payments to them instead of your servicer

Report foreclosure scams to the California Attorney General at (800) 952-5225.
