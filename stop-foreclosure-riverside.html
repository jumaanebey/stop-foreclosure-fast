The timeline, locally

Two counties, one clock

The recording that starts it

Your foreclosure formally begins when the Notice of Default is recorded — with the Riverside County Assessor-County Clerk-Recorder, or the San Bernardino County Recorder if you're in Ontario, Fontana, Rialto, or up the Cajon Pass. Either way, California law then gives you at least 90 days with the right to reinstate and stop the process by bringing the loan current.

Auction no sooner than 21 days after notice

A Notice of Trustee's Sale follows, setting an auction date at least 21 days out at the location printed on it. Between recording and auction there are real intervention points — a complete modification application, an active escrow, a short-sale package — and trustees postpone sales for them routinely.

Appreciation cuts both ways

If you bought before the IE's recent run-up, you may owe far less than the home is worth — which makes an auction, where that difference can evaporate, the worst possible outcome. The full stage-by-stage breakdown is on the California foreclosure timeline.

HUD-approved housing counselors — free

Call (800) 569-4287 for HUD-approved counseling agencies serving Riverside and San Bernardino counties. Free, legitimate, and especially important in a region where foreclosure-rescue scams have a long history of targeting homeowners in default.

Court self-help and Inland legal aid

The Riverside and San Bernardino Superior Courts operate free self-help centers, and Inland Empire legal aid organizations provide free housing-law assistance to eligible residents of both counties.
