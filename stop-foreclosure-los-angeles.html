The timeline, locally

How the California timeline plays out in LA County

Notice of Default — recorded with the county

Foreclosure in Los Angeles County officially begins when your lender's trustee records a Notice of Default with the LA County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk. That recording starts a minimum 90-day period during which you have a statutory right to reinstate — catch up the missed payments plus fees and stop the process entirely.

Notice of Trustee's Sale — at least 21 days before auction

If the 90 days pass without a resolution, a Notice of Trustee's Sale can be recorded and posted, setting an auction date no sooner than 21 days out. The sale location is printed on the notice itself — read it carefully, because that date is the real deadline everything else works backward from.

Even close to the sale date, options remain

Postponements happen every week in a county this size — a complete loan modification application under review, an accepted offer in escrow, or a short-sale package in front of the trustee are all common reasons. LA County is the most populous county in the country, which means the machinery is busy but also that the people who work in it see these requests constantly. Free help in LA and statewide

Resources you should know about

HUD-approved housing counselors — free

Call (800) 569-4287 to reach a HUD-approved counseling agency. Los Angeles County has one of the largest concentrations of these agencies in the state, and their advice costs nothing. I'll never talk you out of a second opinion — it's your best defense against the scams that circle foreclosure.

LA County court self-help and consumer resources

The Los Angeles Superior Court operates free self-help centers — including at the Stanley Mosk Courthouse downtown — that can explain court procedures at no cost. The LA County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs also offers free foreclosure-prevention counseling to county residents.
